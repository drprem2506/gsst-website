What's behind the simulation

The numbers are illustrative, set to show the patterns clearly. The links between them come from research:

  • Fatigue: output rises more slowly than hours once people work beyond about 49 hours a week, and long hours are linked to more accidents and errors (John Pencavel, The Productivity of Working Hours, 2015).
  • Trust and candour: teams that feel safe to speak up report problems and mistakes earlier (Amy Edmondson's research on psychological safety).
  • Rework: errors found late, and redone, are at the heart of why projects slip (system dynamics research on the "rework cycle", reviewed by James Lyneis and David Ford, 2007).
  • Late changes: about half of projects experience scope creep or uncontrolled changes (Project Management Institute, Pulse of the Profession, 2018).
  • Extra control: signals of distrust can lower people's effort (Armin Falk and Michael Kosfeld, "The Hidden Cost of Control", 2006).
  • Adding people: new people need time and support before they add capacity (Fred Brooks, The Mythical Man-Month, 1975).
